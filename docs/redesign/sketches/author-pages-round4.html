Round 4: author pages with a tint

Each author's page takes one muted ground colour and a darker link colour of the same hue. The home page and the reader stay neutral. All tints pass AA contrast for body text and links.

The plays are listed without boxes, in registry order, with the Greek title, the repo's blurb and its production note. The Oresteia is grouped on Aeschylus' page. Only Euripides gets a Find field. The play drawings are not made yet, so the list has none.

Αἰσχύλος

Aeschylus

c. 525–456 BC · 7 plays

The earliest of the three great Athenian tragedians, seven of whose plays survive complete.

Titles and notesTitles only
  • OresteiaThree plays staged together · City Dionysia, 458 BC · first prize
    1. First play
      AgamemnonἈγαμέμνων

      Agamemnon returns from Troy in triumph, and his wife Clytaemestra kills him.

    2. Second play
      Libation BearersΧοηφόροι

      Orestes returns from exile and kills his mother Clytaemestra to avenge his father.

    3. Third play
      EumenidesΕὐμενίδες

      The Furies hound Orestes to Athens, where Athena convenes the first murder trial.

  • PersiansΠέρσαι

    News of the rout at Salamis reaches the Persian court, and Xerxes comes home in rags.

    City Dionysia, 472 BC · first prize

  • Seven Against ThebesἙπτὰ ἐπὶ Θήβας

    Eteocles holds Thebes against his brother, and the two kill each other at the seventh gate.

    City Dionysia, 467 BC · first prize

  • Suppliant WomenἹκέτιδες

    Danaus and his fifty daughters flee forced marriage and beg Argos for shelter.

    City Dionysia, c. 463 BC · first prize

  • Prometheus BoundΠρομηθεὺς δεσμώτης

    Chained to a crag for stealing fire, Prometheus defies Zeus and foretells his fall.

    Date unknown · authorship disputed

Σοφοκλῆς

Sophocles

c. 497–406 BC · 7 plays

Athenian tragedian; seven plays survive of well over a hundred.

Titles and notesTitles only
  • Oedipus cycleThree plays on Oedipus and his children, written years apart and not staged together. Listed in story order.
    1. Oedipus TyrannusΟἰδίπους Τύραννος

      Oedipus hunts Laius' killer through Thebes and finds it was himself: he killed his father and married his mother.

    2. Oedipus at ColonusΟἰδίπους ἐπὶ Κολωνῷ

      Blind and exiled, Oedipus comes to rest in a grove outside Athens as his sons war over Thebes.

    3. AntigoneἈντιγόνη

      Antigone defies Creon's edict and buries her brother Polynices at the cost of her life.

  • AjaxΑἴας

    Shamed by his madness and the slaughter of the army's cattle, Ajax turns on himself.

    Date unknown, probably the 440s BC

  • Women of TrachisΤραχίνιαι

    Deianeira tries to win Heracles back with a love charm, and poisons him.

    Date unknown

  • ElectraἨλέκτρα

    Electra waits years for her brother Orestes to come home and avenge their father.

    Date unknown, probably c. 420–410 BC

  • PhiloctetesΦιλοκτήτης

    Abandoned on Lemnos with a festering wound, Philoctetes is courted for his fatal bow.

    City Dionysia, 409 BC · first prize

Εὐριπίδης

Euripides

c. 480–406 BC · 19 plays

Athenian tragedian; nineteen plays survive, the largest body of Greek drama.

Titles and notesTitles only
  • AlcestisἌλκηστις

    Alcestis agrees to die in her husband Admetus' place, until Heracles wrestles Death for her.

    City Dionysia, 438 BC · second prize, behind Sophocles · fourth play of its group, in place of a satyr play

  • MedeaΜήδεια

    Abandoned by Jason for a royal marriage, Medea kills his bride and their own two sons.

    City Dionysia, 431 BC · third prize, behind Euphorion and Sophocles

  • Children of HeraclesἩρακλεῖδαι

    Heracles' children seek refuge at Athens from Eurystheus, who hunts them into exile.

    Date unknown, c. 430 BC

  • HippolytusἹππόλυτος

    Phaedra's hidden love for her stepson Hippolytus kills them both: her by her own hand, him by his father's curse.

    City Dionysia, 428 BC · first prize

  • AndromacheἈνδρομάχη

    Hector's widow Andromache, now a captive concubine, is hunted by her master's jealous wife.

    Date unknown, c. 425 BC · the scholia say it was not produced at Athens

  • HecubaἙκάβη

    The fallen queen of Troy loses a daughter to sacrifice and a son to murder, and blinds his murderer.

    c. 424 BC

  • Suppliant WomenἹκέτιδες

    The mothers of the fallen Argive champions beg Theseus to force Thebes to yield their dead.

    c. 423 BC

  • ElectraἨλέκτρα

    Married off to a poor farmer, Electra plots with Orestes to kill their mother.

    Date disputed, c. 420–413 BC

  • HeraclesἩρακλῆς

    Heracles returns from his labours to save his family, and Hera drives him mad.

    c. 416 BC

  • Trojan WomenΤρῳάδες

    The morning after Troy's fall, its women learn whose they are, and Astyanax is thrown from the walls.

    City Dionysia, 415 BC · second prize, behind Xenocles

  • Iphigenia in TaurisἸφιγένεια ἡ ἐν Ταύροις

    Saved from sacrifice at Aulis, Iphigenia serves a foreign goddess until her brother arrives.

    c. 414 BC

  • IonἼων

    A temple boy at Delphi learns his true parentage in a plot that nearly costs his mother's life.

    Date disputed, c. 418–410 BC

  • HelenἙλένη

    Helen was never at Troy; Menelaus finds her in Egypt, and they trick their way out.

    City Dionysia, 412 BC · produced with Andromeda

  • Phoenician WomenΦοίνισσαι

    Oedipus' sons Eteocles and Polynices bring their war for Thebes to their mother Jocasta's door.

    c. 409 BC

  • OrestesὈρέστης

    Days after killing his mother, Orestes waits on trial for his life, surrounded by enemies.

    City Dionysia, 408 BC

  • BacchaeΒάκχαι

    Dionysus comes to Thebes to punish Pentheus for denying his divinity, and Pentheus's own mother tears him apart.

    Produced posthumously at the City Dionysia, 405 BC, by Euripides the younger · first prize

  • Iphigenia at AulisἸφιγένεια ἡ ἐν Αὐλίδι

    Agamemnon lures Iphigenia to Aulis with a false marriage to sacrifice her for a fair wind.

    Produced posthumously at the City Dionysia, 405 BC, by Euripides the younger · first prize

  • RhesusῬῆσος

    A night raid on the Trojan camp cuts short the newly arrived ally Rhesus and his Thracian troop.

    Date unknown · authorship disputed

  • CyclopsΚύκλωψ

    Odysseus and his men land on Polyphemus' island in this satyr-play retelling of the Odyssey.

    Satyr play · date unknown, perhaps c. 408 BC

Ἀριστοφάνης

Aristophanes

c. 446–386 BC · 11 plays

The master of Old Comedy, eleven of whose plays survive complete.

Titles and notesTitles only
  • AcharniansἈχαρνῆς

    A farmer makes his own peace with Sparta, and the men of Acharnae want him dead for it.

    Lenaia, 425 BC · first prize

  • KnightsἹππῆς

    A sausage-seller out-demagogues the Paphlagonian, Cleon in all but name, and Demos comes out young again.

    Lenaia, 424 BC · first prize

  • CloudsΝεφέλαι

    Strepsiades sends his son to Socrates to learn how to cheat his creditors, and burns the school down.

    City Dionysia, 423 BC · third prize · the surviving text is a later revision, never staged

  • WaspsΣφῆκες

    A juror who cannot stop convicting is locked in the house by his son.

    Lenaia, 422 BC · second prize

  • PeaceΕἰρήνη

    Trygaeus rides a dung-beetle to heaven to dig up Peace.

    City Dionysia, 421 BC · second prize

  • BirdsὌρνιθες

    Two Athenians build a city in the sky and starve the gods into surrender.

    City Dionysia, 414 BC · second prize

  • LysistrataΛυσιστράτη

    The women of Greece go on sex strike to end the war, and it works.

    411 BC, probably at the Lenaia · result unknown

  • Women at the ThesmophoriaΘεσμοφοριάζουσαι

    Euripides' kinsman infiltrates the women's festival in drag and is caught.

    411 BC, probably at the City Dionysia · result unknown

  • FrogsΒάτραχοι

    Dionysus goes to Hades for a poet, judges Aeschylus against Euripides, and brings Aeschylus back.

    Lenaia, 405 BC · first prize · restaged, the hypothesis says, for its parabasis

  • AssemblywomenἘκκλησιάζουσαι

    The women take over the assembly and make property and sex common to all.

    c. 392 BC · festival and result unknown

  • WealthΠλοῦτος

    Wealth is blind, and Chremylus has him cured.

    388 BC · festival and result unknown
