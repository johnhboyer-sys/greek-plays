# Design decisions: Greek Drama Reader redesign

## Round 1 (2026-10-01): eight sketches

- Keep: the frieze (sketch 1). The four playwrights are divided like figures on a vase.
- Keep: a bold layout (sketch 2), but not huge Greek names.
- Useful but too much for a home page: the full list of 44 titles (sketch 4). Might make separate playwright pages unnecessary.
- Drawings over photos. The photos in sketch 5 felt disjointed.
- No dark ground (sketch 3).
- No timeline on the home page (sketch 6).
- No split by genre (sketch 7). It leaves comedy unbalanced with only one comic author.
- No first lines as the lead (sketch 8). It does not say plainly what the site is.

## Round 2: frieze variations

Same frieze in each. Only the amount under the ground line changes:

- A: names, count, dates, three play links
- B: bolder English names, Greek at a middle size
- C: first four titles and an "All N plays" link
- D: every title

## Round 2 verdicts

- B over A: the English name leads and the Greek sits above it, smaller.
- Either every title or none. A handful of play links implies those are the only plays.
- D's phone layout did not work.
- Drawings: whole figures only, with no hard crop edges. No nudity.
- Reference to look at: the University of Michigan Press Aristophanes covers.

## Round 3

- A: names and play count only
- B: every title, with a new phone layout (one author per row, titles in two columns)

## Round 3 verdicts

- A is simpler. B helps a reader who doesn't know who wrote which play.
- With every title on the home page, separate author pages may be unnecessary (open question).
- Each author gets one muted tint, used on his own pages only. The home page and the reader stay neutral.

## Round 4

- Author pages with tints: Aeschylus slate (#e2e7ec / #34536b), Sophocles sage (#e3e8df / #43603b), Euripides clay (#efe2dc / #8a3f30), Aristophanes ochre (#f0e7d2 / #6e5114). All pass AA.

## Round 4 verdicts

- Author pages: good.
- No side bar for the Oresteia (reads as a blockquote). Now: a heading with one shared production note, and the three plays side by side, each labelled first, second or third play. Stacked on a phone.
- Phone: no production line (date, festival, prize) under the summary. Desktop keeps it.
- Aeschylus: the Oresteia comes first, then the other four plays.
- Sophocles: the Oedipus cycle comes first, in story order (Oedipus Tyrannus, Oedipus at Colonus, Antigone), with a note that the plays were written years apart and not staged together. Then the other four.

## Open, to decide later

- Order of plays on author pages beyond the two groups: date of first performance, or "start here" picks.
- Euripides grouped by myth. Draft: Other stories (Medea, Hippolytus, Ion); Heracles (Alcestis, Heracles, Children of Heracles); Thebes (Bacchae, Phoenician Women, Suppliant Women); the Trojan War (Iphigenia at Aulis, Rhesus, Hecuba, Trojan Women, Andromache, Helen, Cyclops); the house of Agamemnon (Electra, Orestes, Iphigenia in Tauris).
- Aristophanes grouped by subject. Draft: Politics and the war (Acharnians, Knights, Wasps, Peace, Lysistrata); Poets and thinkers (Clouds, Women at the Thesmophoria, Frogs); Imagined cities (Birds, Assemblywomen, Wealth).
- Home-page collections across authors. Candidates: the Electra story three times; the war of Oedipus' sons twice; Euripides in Aristophanes; the women of Troy; Heracles across the playwrights; the two Suppliant Women and the two Iphigenias.
- The University of Michigan Aristophanes covers as a reference (need a screenshot).

## Home page (decided)

- Like round 3 A: the author frieze with names and play counts, linking to author pages. Collections and other resources go underneath the frieze.

## Play opening

- Sketch 2 (playbill): the play on the left, the cast and facts on the right.
- Every play gets its own drawing.
- The drawing is never cropped by the header.
- Persons in two columns.
- List the Greek texts and the translations in full, each with its year. Each translation notes which Greek text it follows, where the repo records it.
- Data gaps: per-play years for Murray's verse translations; Johnston and Kosmos carry only date ranges.
- Revised playbill approved as the starting point for the play opening.

## Reading view

- Three sketches on Agamemnon 1202–1213 and Medea 1271–1292: a shared speaker gutter; names at the start of each speech in each column; Greek first with the translation beside it (on phones, shown speech by speech).
- The reading view stays neutral, with no author tint.
- English is never smaller than the Greek. Readers who translate and readers who read the English with the Greek beside it are roughly equal in number. Same size, same ink.
- Reading view: sketch 2. Speaker names run into the first line of each speech in both columns, with no separate gutter. The Greek and English of each speech start on the same line.
- The author's tint goes in the reader's running head. The text area stays neutral.
- Long speeches: no horizontal rules or chunk breaks on desktop. Greek and English run as continuous columns, with small line markers in the English. On a phone, five lines of Greek alternate with their English.
- No horizontal rules in the reading view at all, including under the running head and the speaker bar.
- Reading page colour: warm paper (#fbf9f4), the same for every author, under the author-tinted running head.

## Drawings, phase 1 (2026-10-02)

- Dark mode: the lines stay dark and the figure is filled with clay, as on a red-figure vase. Light lines on a dark ground looked like a photo negative. Approved.
- CC BY-SA sources are fine. A drawing made from one is CC BY-SA too.
- Drawings may show a play's ending (a blinded Oedipus is fine).
- The engraved and lithographed sources gave the most detailed drawings; photos of sparsely drawn vases (Berlin F 2355, Würzburg H 5697) gave too little detail.
- Oedipus, Sophocles' figure: wanted in a more dynamic pose.
- Oedipus killing the Sphinx (British Museum E 696) is out: the play only alludes to it, so it would mislead about the plot. Prefer the play's own action, then backstory the plot turns on.
- Oedipus Tyrannus: the Sicilian krater in Syracuse (Museo Paolo Orsi 66557), read as a scene from the play. Its only open photos (Dan Diffendale) are CC BY-NC-SA 2.0; allowed for this drawing, which carries the same licence.
- Sophocles' home-page figure: the shepherd carrying the infant Oedipus (Paris, Cabinet des Médailles 372), unless a stronger Sophocles figure turns up. Berlin F 2355 lacked detail.
- Oedipus at Colonus: find the vase Taplin lists for the play (Pots and Plays no. 27, the blind Oedipus with his daughters as suppliants).
- Copyright (John, 2026-10-02): "The vase's image ISN'T copyrighted even if the photo IS." The painting on an ancient vase is public domain; a line drawing traced from it may use an in-copyright photo as its source. First use: Oedipus at Colonus, from Taplin's vase (Pots and Plays no. 27, Geddes collection, Melbourne), which John now wants.
- Oedipus Tyrannus: the head was "crude and not refined"; redrawn with the hair solid, the beard hatched, and the face lines even.
- The shepherd with the infant Oedipus: use it ("definitely use that for something"); it stays Sophocles' home-page figure.
- Antigone bound (Berlin F 3240, Darius Painter) goes to a Sophocles play, Antigone, whatever play the vase was painted for.
- The Syracuse Oedipus Tyrannus drawing drops the CC BY-NC-SA licence (John, 2026-10-02: "drop it, with the license"): it traces only the public-domain painting. Diffendale's photo is still credited.
