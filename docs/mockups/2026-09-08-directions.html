Direction A

The Orchestra

The theatre used as structure, not ornament. A limestone page, glaze-black ink, and one band of Attic red for tragedy, ochre for comedy. The plate is a programme: cast as a playbill, the Greek title set large on the band. Choral passages sit on a faintly tinted field behind a hairline rule, so the metre change is visible before it is read.

Direction B

The Manuscript

The codex that carried these plays, done as typography and nothing else: no textures, no parchment. Cardo for both languages, a cool vellum ground, line numbers and notes set in the margin like scholia. Speaker colours come from the pigments of a scriptorium (ultramarine, verdigris, Tyrian, ochre, carbon, madder), the rubric red is tragedy's accent and malachite green is comedy's. Odes narrow their measure and turn on a small ornament.

Direction C

The Score

Drama as notation. Line number, metre tag and speaker sit in a fixed gutter grid, the Greek in STIX Two, the English in Plex Serif, everything else in Plex Mono. Colour is cool and scarce: a deep wine for tragedy, a green-black for comedy, cast hues desaturated so they organise rather than decorate. The plate is a title card on the accent ground in both themes. This is the direction a scansion layer drops into without a redesign; the gutter is already waiting for it.
