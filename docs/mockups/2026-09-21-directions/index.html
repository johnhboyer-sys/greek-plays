1. Directions inside a speech

Two shapes occur. Orestes 1288–91: Perseus marks delivery with a bare sung and spoken in the middle of Electra's speech. Hecuba 432–37 and 438–43: a full sentence of stage business closes the speech. 2. Medea's sons

The “Persons” list on Medea's plate names three sons, in three colours, for two boys. Diggle prints three sigla at the murder: ΠΑΙΣ (ἔσωθεν) for the first cry at 1270a, then Πα.α and Πα.β. Coleridge heads the first cry “Sons”. The list below is the plate as built; under it, the three lines as the reader shows them.

3. A direction that stands where a speech would go

Libation Bearers 869. Aegisthus cries out inside the palace: ἒ ἔ, ὀτοτοτοῖ. Smyth and Morshead give him words (“Oh! Oh! O woe!”). Johnston gives him none: he prints only a direction, [Aegisthus screams in pain from inside the palace]. The question is where that direction sits in Johnston's column.
