PLUTUS

[SCENE—The front of a farm-house with a road leading to it. A blind old man is seen followed at some distance by CHREMYLUS and his servant CARIO.]

CARIO. How troublesome[1] a thing it is, O Jupiter and ye gods, to be the slave of a crazy master! For if the servant should happen to have given the best advice, and it should seem fit to his master not to do this, it must be that the servant share the evils;[2] for fortune suffers not the natural owner to be master of his person, but the purchaser. And so much for this.[3] But Loxias[4] who prophesies from his tripod of beaten gold I censure with this just censure, because being a physician and a clever soothsayer, as they say, he has sent away my master melancholy-mad,[5] who is following behind[6] a blind man, acting contrary to what it became him to do; for we who see lead the blind; whereas he follows him, and compels me besides; and that too without even[7] answering a syllable[8] at all. Therefore it is not possible for me to hold my
tongue, unless you tell me, master, for what in the world we are following this man, but I'll give you trouble; for you will not beat[9] me while I wear the chaplet.
Chr. No, by Jove, but if you trouble me in any way, I'll do it when I have taken away your chaplet, that you may grieve the more.
Ca. Nonsense! for I will not cease until you tell me who in the world this is; for I ask it, being exceedingly well[10] disposed to you.
Chr. Well then, I will not hide it from you, for I do believe you to be the most faithful of my domestics, and—the arrantest thief.[11] I, though a religious and just man, was unprosperous and poor.
Ca. In truth I know it.
Chr. While others, sacrilegious persons, demagogues, and informers, and villains, were rich.
Ca. I believe[12] you.
Chr. So I went to the god to consult him, thinking that my own life,[13] unhappy man, had now nearly been wasted away, but to ask about my son,[14] who is my only one,[15] if he
ought to change his habits and be knavish, unjust, nothing good ;[16] since I thought this very thing to be advantageous for life.

CARIO. What then did Phoebus proclaim from amongst his chaplets ?[17]

CHREMYLUS. You shall hear : for the god told me this plainly ; whomsoever I should first meet with[18] on going out,[19] him he bid me[20] never let go, but prevail on him to accompany me home.

CARIO. And whom then did you first meet with ?

CHREMYLUS. With this man.

CARIO. Then did you not understand the meaning of the god, when it directed you, O most stupid, in the plainest terms, to educate[21] your son after the fashion of the country ?

CHREMYLUS. By what[22] do you judge of this ?

CARIO. It is evident that even a blind man fancies he knows this,[23] that it is very advantageous to practise no virtue in these times.

CHREMYLUS. It is not possible that the oracle inclines to this,[24] but to something else of greater moment. But if this fellow tell us who in the world he is, and on account of what, and in want of what he came hither with us, we might understand what our oracle[25] means.

CARIO. (to Plutus). Come now, do you declare yourself, who you are, before I do what comes next.[26] You must be very quick about speaking.

PLUTUS. A plague take you![27]

CARIO. (to Chrem.). Do you understand whom he professes himself to be?

CHREMYLUS. He says this to you, not to me; for you inquire of him uncouthly and roughly. But [to Plutus] if you take any pleasure in the manners of a man of honour, tell me!

PLUTUS. Go, hang yourself!

CARIO. Take[28] the man, and omen of the god.

CHREMYLUS. By Ceres, you certainly shall not any longer escape[29] unpunished!

CARIO. For unless you will tell us, I will kill you, you wretch, in a wretched way.[30]

PLUTUS. Good sirs, depart from me.

CHREMYLUS. Not a whit.[31]

CARIO. Well now, what I say, is best, master: I'll kill this fellow in a most wretched way; for I will set him up on[32] some precipice and leave him and go away, that he may fall and break his neck.

CHREMYLUS. Well, up with him quickly.

PLUTUS. By no means.

CHREMYLUS. Will you not tell us then?

PLUTUS. But if you learn who I am, I well know that you will do me some mischief, and not let me go.[33]

CHREMYLUS. By the gods will we, if you wish it.

PLUTUS. Then first let me go.

CHREMYLUS. Lo! we let you go.

PLUTUS. Hear now; for, as it seems, I must speak what I was[34] prepared to conceal: I am Plutus.

CHREMYLUS. O most abominable of all men! did you hold your tongue then, you Plutus?[35]

CARIO. You Plutus, so wretchedly circumstanced?

CHREMYLUS. O Phœbus Apollo, and ye gods and dæmons, and Jove, what do you say? Are you really he?

PLUTUS. Yes.

CHREMYLUS. He himself?

PLUTUS. His very self.[36]

CHREMYLUS. Whence then, tell us, come you so squalid?

PLUTUS. I come from the house of Patrocles,[37] who has not washed himself since he was born.

CHREMYLUS. But how did you suffer this mishap?[38] Declare it to me.

PLUTUS. Jupiter treated me in this manner through envy towards mankind. For when I was a boy,[39] I threatened that I
would[40] go to the just, and[41] wise, and well-behaved alone. So he made me blind, that I might not distinguish any of these. So much does he envy the good.

CHREMYLUS. And yet he is honoured by the good and the just alone.

PLUTUS. I grant you.

CHREMYLUS. Come, what then? if you were to recover your sight again, just as formerly,[42] would you now shun the wicked?

PLUTUS. Certainly.

CHREMYLUS. But would you go to the just?

PLUTUS. Most assuredly; for I have not seen[43] them for a long time.[44]

CHREMYLUS. And no wonder too; for neither have I, who see.

PLUTUS. Now let me go; for now you know all about me.[45]

CHREMYLUS. No, by Jove! but so much the more will we keep hold of you.

PLUTUS. Did I not say that you would[46] cause me trouble?

CHREMYLUS. And do you, I beseech you, comply, and do not abandon me; for you will never[47] find a man better in his morals than I, if you search. No, by Jove! for there is no other[48] save me.

PLUTUS. They all say this: but when they actually get possession of me, and become wealthy, they absolutely exceed all bounds in their wickedness.

CHREMYLUS. So it is: yet all are not wicked.

PLUTUS. No, by Jove, not all, but all without exception.[49]

CARIO. You shall suffer[50] for it severely.

CHREMYLUS. And that you may know how many blessings you will have, if you stay with us, give your attention, that you may hear. For I think, I think,—with god's permission[51] it shall be spoken,—that I shall free you from this[52] blindness, having made you see.

PLUTUS. By no means do this; for I do not wish to recover my sight again.

CHREMYLUS. What do you say?

CARIO. This fellow is a born[53] miserable.

PLUTUS. I know indeed that Jupiter would destroy me, if he were to hear of the follies of these men.[54]

CHREMYLUS. But does he not do this now, who suffers you to go about stumbling?[55]

PLUTUS. I know not; but I dread him exceedingly.

CHREMYLUS. What really, O you most cowardly of all deities?[56] For do you suppose the sovereignty[57] of Jove and his thunderbolts would be worth a three-obol piece,[58] if you should recover your sight, if it were but for a short time?

PLUTUS. Ah! say not so, you wretch!

CHREMYLUS. Be quiet;[59] for I will demonstrate you to be far more powerful than Jupiter.

PLUTUS. Me?[60]

CHREMYLUS. Aye, by heaven. For, for example,[61] through whom does Jupiter rule the gods?

CARIO. Through money, for he has most of it.

CHREMYLUS. Come, who then is it that supplies[62] him with this?

CARIO. This person here.

CHREMYLUS. And through whom do men sacrifice to him? is it not through him?

CARIO. And, by Jupiter, they pray openly[63] to be rich.

CHREMYLUS. Is not he then the cause, and might he not easily put an end to this, if he wished?

PLUTUS. Why so?[64] why pray?

CHREMYLUS. Because[65] no man would any longer sacrifice, either ox or barley-cake, or any thing else whatever, if you were not willing.

PLUTUS. How?

CHREMYLUS. How? it is not possible for him to purchase it, I ween, unless you yourself be present[66] and give him the money; so that you alone will put down the power of Jove, if he annoy you in any way.

PLUTUS. What do you say? do they sacrifice to him through me?

CHREMYLUS. Certainly. And, by Jupiter, if there be any thing magnificent and beautiful or agreeable to men,[67] it is through you: for all things are subservient[68] to riches.

CARIO. I, in truth, have become a slave on account of a trifling sum of money,[69] because I was not equally rich as others.

CHREMYLUS. And they say that the Corinthian courtesans, when any poor man tries them, do not even pay any attention to him, but if a rich man try, that they immediately turn any thing to[70] him.

CARIO. And they say that the boys do this very thing, not for their lovers', but the money's sake.

CHREMYLUS. Not the better sort, but the catamites; for the better sort do not ask for money.

CARIO. What then?[71]

CHREMYLUS. One asks for a good horse, another hunting dogs.[72]

CARIO. For, perhaps, being ashamed to ask for money, they gloss over[73] their wickedness by a false name.

CHREMYLUS. And all arts and clever contrivances among men have been invented through you. For one of them sits and makes shoes; and some other one is a smith,[74] and another a carpenter; another is a goldsmith, having received gold from you; another, by Jove, steals clothes; another is a housebreaker; another is a fuller;[75] another washes fleeces; another is a tanner; another sells onions; another, having been detected as an adulterer, is depillated through you.

PLUTUS. Ah me, miserable! this has been unknown to me this long while.

CARIO. And does not the Great King pride himself through him? And is not the Assembly held through him? But how? —do you not man the triremes? tell me. And does not he support the mercenaries in Corinth?[76] And will not Pamphilus[77] suffer through him? And will not the “Needle-seller”[78] along with Pamphilus? And does not Agyrrhius[79] fart through
him? And [to Plutus] does not Philepsius[80] relate fables on account of you? And is not the alliance[81] with the Egyptians through you. And does not Lais, through you, love Philonides?[82] And the tower of Timotheus[83]———

CHREMYLUS. ———May it fall upon you. And [to Plutus] are not all our affairs transacted through you? For you alone[84] are the cause of all, both of our miseries and our blessings, be well assured.

CARIO. At any rate, in wars also, they always conquer, upon whom he only sits down.[85]

PLUTUS. Am I able, single as I am, to effect so many things?

CHREMYLUS. And, by Jupiter, far more than these; so that no one[86]

PLUTUS. Take care!

PLUTUS. Deary me! you tell us of miserable allies.

CHREMYLUS. Not so, if they become rich again as before. But do you [to Cario] go and run quickly—

CARIO. What am I to do? Tell me.

CHREMYLUS. Call my fellow-labourers,—and you will probably find them working hard in the fields,—that each, being present here, may share an equal portion with us of this Plutus.

PLUTUS. But, by the gods, I am exceedingly loth to be always going into other people's houses. For I never at any time got any good from it.[101] For if I chance to go into the house of a miser, he immediately buries[102] me deep in the earth: and if any good man, his friend, come to him asking to get some small sum of money, he denies that he has ever at any time even seen me. But if I chance to go into the house of a mad fellow, I am exposed to harlots and dice and driven out of doors naked in a moment of time.[103]

CHREMYLUS. Yes; for you never at any time met with[104] a moderate man. But I am somehow always of this character.[105] For I both take pleasure in saving, as never man did, and again in spending,[106] whenever there is occasion for it. But let us go in; for I wish both my wife to see you[107] and my only son, whom I love most of all—next to you.

PLUTUS. I believe you.

CHREMYLUS. For why should one not tell the truth to you?[108]
[Exeunt Chremylus and Plutus.]

CARIO. Oh you who have often eaten of the same thyme[109] with my master, his friends, and fellow-tribesmen, and lovers of labour, come, make haste, hurry, since the time does not admit delay, but it is at the very crisis at which you ought to be present[110] and lend your aid.
CHORUS OF COUNTRY-PEOPLE.[111] Don't you see then that we have been actively hastening this long while, as is reasonable those should who are now feeble old men? But you, perhaps, expect that I should run, before you even tell me this,[112] on what account your master has called me hither.

CARIO. Have I not then, I ween, been telling you this long while? It is you yourself that don't hear. For my master says that you shall all of you live pleasantly, freed from your dreary and unpleasant mode of life.

CHORUS. But what, pray, and whence, is this thing which he speaks of?

CARIO. He has come hither with a certain old man, ye wretches, who is filthy, crooked, miserable, wrinkled, bald, and toothless; and, by heaven, I think he is circumcised,[113] too.

CHORUS. O you who have announced golden tidings,[114] how say
you? tell me again! For you plainly show that he is come with a heap of money.

CARIO. Nay, rather, with [115] a heap of the ills of age.

CHORUS. Do you expect, after humbugging us, to get off unpunished, and that, too, when I have a staff?

CARIO. Why, do you consider me to be altogether such a man by nature in all respects, and do you think that I would say [116] nothing true?

CHORUS. How haughty the rascal [117] is! Your legs are crying out, "Oh! Oh!" longing for the stocks and fetters.

CARIO. But are you not for going, when now your letter [118] has assigned [119] you to administer justice in the tomb, and Charon [120] gives you your ticket [121]?

CHORUS. Split you! [122] What an impudent fellow you are, and arrant knave by nature, who [123] humbug us, and have not yet had the patience to tell us on what account your master has called me hither, who, [124] after labouring much, have come hither readily, though we had no leisure, passing over [125] many roots of thyme.

CARIO. Well then, I will not conceal it any longer; for, sirs, my master has come with Plutus, who will make you rich.

CHORUS. Why, is it really possible for us all to be rich?

CARIO. Nay, rather, by the gods, all Midases, [126] if you get ass's ears.

CHORUS. How I am delighted and gladdened, and wish to dance for joy, if you are really speaking [127] this truly.

CARIO. Well now, I should like [128] to lead you, imitating [129] the Cyclops, threttanelo! and moving thus to and fro with my feet. CHORUS. CARIO. But come now, do you now have done with your jests and turn yourselves into another shape;[136] while I should like now to go unknown to my master and take some bread and meat and eat it, and so afterwards to join in the work. [Exit Cario.]

CHORUS. Be of good courage! for you shall think I look downright martial.[140] For it would be absurd,[141] if we constantly jostle one another in the Assembly for the sake of three obols, while I were to yield up Plutus himself to any one to take away.[142]
CHr. Well now, I see also Blepsidemus here approaching: and ’tis plain[143] from his gait and haste that he has heard something of the affair. [Enter Blepsidemus.]

BLEPSIDEMUS. (talking to himself). What then can the affair be? whence and in what way has Chremylus suddenly become rich? I don’t believe it: and yet, by Hercules, there was much talk among those who sat in the barbers’ shops,[144] that the man has suddenly become wealthy. But this very thing is marvellous to me, that he, being well off, sends for his friends. In truth he does not[145] do a thing fashionable in the country.
CHr. (aside). Well then, by the gods, I’ll tell him, without concealing any thing.[146] O Blepsidemus, we are better off than yesterday, so that it is permitted you to share; for you are of the number of my friends.[147]

BLEPSIDEMUS. But have you really become[148] rich, as people say?

CHREMYLUS. Nay, but I shall be very soon, if God please;[149] for there is—there is some hazard in the affair.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Of what sort?[150]

CHREMYLUS. Such as—

BLEPSIDEMUS. Tell me quickly[151] what in the world you mean.[152]
CHR.—that, if we succeed, we shall be always well off;[153] but if we be foiled, we shall be utterly undone.

BLEPSIDEMUS. This load[154] looks bad, and does not please me. For your suddenly becoming so excessively rich, and, again, your fearing, is in character with a man[155] who has done nothing good.

CHREMYLUS. How nothing good?

BLEPSIDEMUS. If, by Jove, you have come from thence, having stolen any silver or gold from the god, and then, perhaps, repent.

CHREMYLUS. O Apollo, averter of evil! not I, by Jove!

BLEPSIDEMUS. Cease talking nonsense, my good sir; for I know it for certain.

CHREMYLUS. Do you suspect nothing of the kind[156] of me.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Alas! how there is absolutely no good[157] in any one! but all are slaves[158] of gain.
Chr. By Ceres, you certainly do not appear to me to be in your right senses.
Bl. (aside). How much he has altered[159] from the character[160] he formerly had!
Chr. By heaven, fellow, you are mad!
Bl. (aside). But not even does his glance itself keep[161] in its place, but is like[162] to one who has committed some villany.
Chr. I know what you are croaking[163] about: you seek to get a share, as if I had stolen something.
Bl. I seek to get a share? of what?
Chr. Whereas[164] it is not of such nature, but different.[165]
Bl. Have you not stolen, but snatched it away?[166]
Chr. You are possessed.

Chr. Not I, indeed![167]

BLEPSIDEMUS. My good friend, I will[169] settle this for you at a very trifling expense, before the city hear of it, by stopping the orators' mouths[170] with small coin.

CHREMYLUS. Certainly.

CHREMYLUS. Yes, by the gods!

BLEPSIDEMUS. Are you speaking truth?

CHREMYLUS. Yea, by Neptune!

BLEPSIDEMUS. Then are you not for sending him round to us also, your friends?

CHREMYLUS. The affair is not yet come to this point.[179]

BLEPSIDEMUS. What do you say? not to the sharing[180] point—eh?

CHREMYLUS. No, by Jupiter! for we must first——

CHREMYLUS. Cause him to see.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Whom to see? tell me.

CHREMYLUS. Plutus, as before, in some way or other.[181]

BLEPSIDEMUS. Why, is he really blind?

CHREMYLUS. Yes, by heaven!

BLEPSIDEMUS. No wonder,[182] then, he never at any time came to me.

CHREMYLUS. But, if the gods please, he shall come now.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Ought[183] you not then to call in some physician?

CHREMYLUS. What physician then is there now in the city? For neither is the fee of any value,[184] nor the profession.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Let us see.

CHREMYLUS. But there is none.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Neither do I think so.

CHREMYLUS. No, by Jupiter; but 'tis best to lay him on a couch in[185] the temple of Æsculapius,[186] as I was intending this long while.[187]

BLEPSIDEMUS. Nay, rather, far the best, by the gods. Do not then delay, but make haste and do something or other.

CHREMYLUS. Well now, I am going.

BLEPSIDEMUS. Hasten then.

CHREMYLUS. I am doing this very thing. [Enter Poverty.]
Pov. O you pitiful mannikins, who dare to do[188] a hasty and unholy and unlawful deed! whither? whither? why do you fly? will you not remain?

Pov. I will destroy you, you wretches, in a wretched[189] way; for you are venturing on a daring act not to be borne, but such as no other person even at any time, either[190] god or man, has ventured on; therefore you are undone.[191]

CHREMYLUS. But who are you? for you appear to me to be ghastly pale.

BLEPSIDEMUS. By no means.

CHREMYLUS. Will you not stay? What! shall we two men fly from one woman?

BLEPSIDEMUS. Yes, for 'tis Poverty, you wretch, than whom there is no living being any where more ruinous.

CHREMYLUS. Stand, I beseech you, stand!

Pov. But first of all, there will not even be any one, not even a kidnapper, according to the statement, I ween, which you mention. For who that is wealthy will be willing to do this[236] at the hazard of his own life? Chr. May it fall on your own head![237]
Pov. Moreover you will not be able to sleep either in a bed,—for there will be none,—or in carpets; for who will be willing to weave them when he has gold? Pov. You are trying to scoff at and ridicule me, heedless of being earnest, not knowing that I render men better both in mind and body than Plutus[253] does. CHREMYLUS. For, no doubt,[255] you bring about the slenderness for them by hunger.
Pov. Now therefore I will discourse to you respecting sobriety, and will demonstrate that orderly behaviour dwells with me, but that riotousness belongs to Plutus.

CHREMYLUS. In sooth it is very orderly to steal and to dig through walls.

And I could not sleep; but a pot of porridge which was lying a little way off from the head of an old woman strongly affected me, towards which I desired exceedingly to creep. Then on looking up I see the priest snatching away[291] the cakes and dried figs from the sacred table. CARIO. No, by Jove, not I; but a dung-eater.[301]

WIFE. How very[314] patriotic and wise the god is!

CARIO. After this he sat down beside Plutus:[315] and first he handled his head, and then he took a clean napkin and wiped his eyelids all round: and Panacca covered his head and the whole of his face with a purple cloth. CHREMYLUS. Certainly not, by Jove, for your good; be well assured.

INFORMER. For, by Jove, you will dine at my cost.

CHREMYLUS. For the sake of truth[370] may you burst, together with your witness, filled with nothing.[371]

INFORMER. Do you deny it? There is a great quantity[372] of slices of salt-fish and roast meat within, you most abominable fellows. [Sniffs.] uhu, uhu, uhu, uhu, uhu, uhu.[373]

CHREMYLUS. Do you smell any thing,[374] you poor wretch?

JUST MAN. The cold, perhaps; since he has on[375] such a threadbare cloak.

INFORMER. Is this bearable[376] then, O Jupiter and ye gods, that these should commit outrages upon me? Ah me! how grieved I am that, good and patriotic as I am, I fare badly.

CHREMYLUS. You patriotic and good?

INFORMER. As never man was.

CHREMYLUS. Well now, answer me when asked—

INFORMER. What?[377]

CHREMYLUS. Are you a husbandman?

INFORMER. Do you suppose me to be so mad?

CHREMYLUS. Or a merchant?[378]

INFORMER. Yes, I pretend to be,[379] upon occasion.

CHREMYLUS. Well then, did you learn any trade?

INFORMER. No, by Jove.

CHREMYLUS. How then, or whence, did you live,[380] if you do nothing?

INFORMER. I am manager of all the affairs of the state and private affairs.
CHr. You? Wherefore?[381]

INFORMER. I please to do so.
CHr. How then, you house-breaker,[382] can you be good, if, when it in no wise concerns you,[383] you are then hated?

INFORMER. Why, does it not concern me,[384] you booby, to benefit my own city as far as I be able?
CHr. Then is to be a meddling busybody to benefit it?

INFORMER. Nay, rather, to aid the established[385] laws, and, if any one do wrong, not to permit it.
CHr. Does not the state, then, purposely appoint judges to preside?

INFORMER. But who is the accuser?
CHr. Any one who pleases.

INFORMER. Then I am he; so that the affairs of the state have devolved on me.
CHr. Then, by Jove, it has a sorry patron. But would you not prefer that,[386] to keep quiet and live idle?

INFORMER. Nay, you are describing the life of a sheep, if there shall appear no amusement in life.
CHr. And would you not learn better?

INFORMER. Not even if you were to give[387] me Plutus himself, and the silphium of Battus.[388]
CHr. Quickly lay down your cloak.

CARIO. (to the informer). Ho you! he is speaking to you.
CHr. Next take off your shoes.

CARIO. (to the informer). He says all this to you.

INFORMER. Well now, let any of you that pleases come hither against me.

CARIO. “Then I am he.” [Seizes the informer and strips him of his cloak and shoes.]

INFORMER. Ah me, miserable! I am stripped in[389] the day time.

CARIO. For you do not hesitate to get a livelihood by meddling with other people’s business.

INFORMER. (to his witness). Do you see what he is doing? I call you to witness this.[390] [His witness runs off.]

CHREMYLUS. But the witness whom[391] you brought is running away.

INFORMER. Ah me, I have been caught alone.

CARIO. Do you bawl now?

INFORMER. Ah me, again and[392] again!

CARIO. Do you [to the Just Man] give me your threadbare cloak, that I may put it on this informer.

JUST MAN. Certainly not; for it has been this long while consecrated to Plutus.

CARIO. Where then will it be better dedicated than around a knavish man and house-breaker? But Plutus it is fitting to adorn with grand dresses.

JUST MAN. But what shall one make of the shoes? tell me.

CARIO. These also I will instantly nail fast to this man’s forehead, as if to a wild[393] olive.

INFORMER. I’ll begone; for I perceive I am much weaker than you. But if I find a comrade, even of fig-tree wood,[394] I will to-day make this powerful god give me satisfaction, because he singly and alone is manifestly putting down the democracy, having neither prevailed upon the Senate of the citizens nor the Assembly.

JUST MAN. Well now, since you are marching with my panoply on, run to the bath, and then stand there in the front and
warm[395] yourself. For I also once held this post. [Exit informer.]

CHREMYLUS. But the bath-man will take and drag him[396] out of doors by the testicles; for when he has seen him he will perceive that he is of that[397] bad stamp. But let us two go in, that you may offer up your vows to the god.[398] [Exeunt Chremylus and Just Man.]

[entering and bearing some cakes on a platter]
OLD WOMAN. O dear old men, have we really come to the house[399] of this new god, or have we altogether missed the road?

CHORUS. Nay, know that you have come to the very door,[400] my little girl; for you ask seasonably.[401]

OLD WOMAN. Come then, let me summon[402] some one of those within. [Enter Chremylus.]

CHREMYLUS. Certainly not;[403] for I myself have come out. But you must tell me for what in particular you have come.

OLD WOMAN. O dearest sir, I have suffered dreadful and unjust things: for since what time this god began[404] to have the use of his eyes, he has made my life to be insupportable.[405]

CHREMYLUS. What's the matter? I suppose you also were an informeress[406] amongst women?

OLD WOMAN. No, by Jupiter, not I.

CHREMYLUS. Or did you not drink in your letter,[407] having obtained it by lot?

OLD WOMAN. You are mocking me ; but I burn with love,[408] unhappy woman.

CHREMYLUS. Will you not then quickly tell me what is your love ?

OLD WOMAN. Hear then ! I had a dear youth, poor, indeed, but, for the rest,[409] good looking, and handsome, and good. For if I wanted anything, he used to perform every thing for me decently and well, while I assisted him in all his wants in the same manner.

CHREMYLUS. But what was it he especially wanted of you, on each occasion ?

OLD WOMAN. Not much ; for he was marvellously respectful to me. But he used to ask for[410] twenty drachmæ of silver for a mantle, and eight for shoes ; and he used to entreat me to purchase a tunic[411] for his sisters, and a little mantle for his mother ; and he used to beg for four medimni of wheat.

CHREMYLUS. Certainly, by Apollo, this is not much which[412] you have mentioned ; but it is evident that he respected you.

OLD WOMAN. And these moreover he said he asked of me, not on account of lewdness,[413] but for affection, that while wearing my mantle, he might think on[414] me.

CHREMYLUS. You describe a man most marvellously[415] in love with you.

OLD WOMAN. But the abominable fellow now no longer has the same mind, but has changed very much. For when I sent him this cheese-cake here and the other[416] sweetmeats which are upon the plate, and whispered[417] that I would come in the evening—

CHREMYLUS. What did he do to you? tell me.

OLD WOMAN. He sent back to us besides this milk-cake here, on condition[418] that I never came thither any more; and besides, in addition to this, when[419] sending it off he said, "Once in olden time the Milesians[420] were brave."

CHREMYLUS. It is evident that he was not very bad[421] in his character. So then,[422] being rich, he no longer takes pleasure in lentil-porridge: but formerly, through his poverty, he used to eat every thing as a relish.[423]

OLD WOMAN. And yet formerly, by the two goddesses,[424] he used always to come to my door every day.

CHREMYLUS. For your burial?

OLD WOMAN. No, by Jupiter, but merely through a desire to hear my voice.

CHREMYLUS. Nay, rather, for the sake of getting something.

OLD WOMAN. And, by Jove, if he perceived me afflicted, he used to call me coaxingly his little duck and little dove.

CHREMYLUS. And then, perhaps, he used to ask [425] you for money for shoes.

OLD WOMAN. And when any one looked at me when riding in my carriage at the Great Mysteries, I was beaten on account of this the whole day; so very jealous was the young man.[426]

CHREMYLUS. For he took pleasure, as it seems, in eating alone.

OLD WOMAN. And he said I had very beautiful[427] hands.

CHREMYLUS. Aye, whenever they offered twenty drachmæ.[428]

OLD WOMAN. And he said I smelt[429] sweet in my skin—

CHREMYLUS. Aye, like enough, by Jove, if you poured in Thasian[430] wine for him.

OLD WOMAN. And that I had a gentle and beautiful look.[431]

CHREMYLUS. The man was no fool, but knew how to devour the substance of a lustful old woman.

OLD WOMAN. In this therefore, O dear sir, the god does not act rightly, who professes to succour whoever happen to be wronged.[432]

CHREMYLUS. Why, what must he do?[433] speak, and it shall be done immediately.[434]

OLD WOMAN. It is just, by Jove, to compel him who has
been benefited by me to benefit me in turn; or he deserves to possess no blessing whatever.

CHREMYLUS. Did he not then repay you every night?

OLD WOMAN. But he said he would never desert me while I lived.

CHREMYLUS. Aye, rightly; but now[435] he thinks you no longer alive.

OLD WOMAN. For I am wasting away through grief, O dearest friend.

CHREMYLUS. No, but you have rotted away, as it appears[436] to me.

OLD WOMAN. Indeed, then, you might draw me through a ring.[437]

CHREMYLUS. Yes, if the ring were the hoop of a sieve.

OLD WOMAN. Well now, see! here's the youth approaching,[438] whom I have been accusing this long while; and he seems to be going to a revel.

CHREMYLUS. He appears so: at least he is certainly coming with a chaplet and a torch.[439] [Enter a young man with a lighted torch in his hand and followed by a band of revellers.]

YOUTH. I salute you.

OLD WOMAN. (to Chremylus). What says he?

YOUTH. My ancient sweetheart, by heaven, you have quickly become gray.

OLD WOMAN. Unhappy me, for the insult with which[440] I am insulted!

CHREMYLUS. He seems to have seen you after a long time.

OLD WOMAN. Since[441] what time, O most audacious, who[442] was at my house yesterday?
Chr. Then he is affected in a manner opposite to most people; for, as it seems, he sees sharper when he's drunk.

OLD WOMAN. No, but he is always saucy in his manners.[443]
You. (holding the torch close to her face). O Sea-Poseidon[444] and ye elderly gods, how many wrinkles[445] she has in her face!

OLD WOMAN. Ah! ah! don't bring the torch near me!
Chr. Upon my word she says rightly; for if only a single spark catch her, it will burn her like an old harvest-wreath.
You. Will you play with me for a[446] while?

OLD WOMAN. Where, wretch?
You. Here, having taken some nuts.

OLD WOMAN. What game?
You. How many teeth[447] you have.
Chr. Come, I also will have a guess; for she has three, perhaps, or four.
You. Pay up! for she carries only one grinder.

OLD WOMAN. Most audacious of men, you don't appear to me to be in your right senses, who make a wash-pot of me[448] in the presence of so many men.

YOUTH. Upon my word you’d be the better for it,[449] if one were to wash you clean.

CHREMYLUS. Certainly not, for now she is playing the cheat :[450] but if this white-lead[451] shall be washed off, you’ll see the wrinkles in her face quite plain.

OLD WOMAN. You don’t appear[452] to me to be in your right senses, old man as you are.

YOUTH. Perhaps, indeed, he is tempting you, and is touching your breasts, fancying that he escapes my notice.

OLD WOMAN. No, by Venus, not mine, you abominable fellow.

CHREMYLUS. No, by Hecate, certainly not! for I should be mad. But, young man, I won’t suffer you to hate this girl.

YOUTH. Nay, I love her beyond measure.

CHREMYLUS. And yet she accuses you.

YOUTH. What does she accuse me of?

CHREMYLUS. She says that you are an insolent person, and that you tell her, “Once in olden time the Milesians were brave.”

YOUTH. I will not quarrel with you about her.

CHREMYLUS. Why so?

YOUTH. Out of respect for your age; for I would never have suffered another to do so; but now go in peace, having taken the girl along with you.

CHREMYLUS. I know, I know your meaning; perhaps you no longer deign to be with her.

OLD WOMAN. But who is there to permit him?[453]
Dobree refers to Bekk. Anecd. i. p. 58, 27. Toup, Suid. iii. p. 101—103.
You. I would not have to do[454] with one who has been embraced by thirteen[455] thousand years.

CHREMYLUS. But yet, since you thought proper to drink the wine, you must also drink[456] up the dregs.
You. But the dregs are altogether old and fusty.

CHREMYLUS. Then a straining-cloth will cure all this.
You. Come, go within! for I wish to go and dedicate to the god these chaplets which I have on.

OLD WOMAN. And I also wish to say something to him.
You. But I will not go in.

CHREMYLUS. Be of good courage, don't be afraid! for she shan't ravish you.
You. Now you say very well: for I have been pitching her up long enough already.[457]

OLD WOMAN. Go in, and I'll enter after you. [Exeunt Old Woman and Young Man.]

CHREMYLUS. How forcibly, O King Jove, the old woman sticks to the youth like a limpet.[458] [Exit Chremylus.]
[Enter Mercury, who knocks at the door, and then runs away, frightened at the noise he had made.]

[from within]
CARIO. Who's that knocking[459] at the door? [Comes out and looks about.] What's this?[460] It appears to be nobody. Then certainly the door shall suffer[461] for creaking without cause. [Retires again.]
who is so free to deliver me up?" Fielding. "Wer denn ist mein Vormund hier?" Droysen. Cf. Pax, 881. Equit. 143.

MERCURY. (running out of his hiding-place). Cario! You, I say![462] stop!

CARIO. (coming out again). Hollo, you! Tell me, did you knock at the door so violently?

MERCURY. No, by Jove; but I was a going to; and then you anticipated me by opening it. Come, run quickly and call out your master, then his wife and children, then his servants, then the dog, then yourself, then the sow.

CARIO. Tell me, what’s the matter?

MERCURY. Jupiter, you rascal, intends to mix you up in the same bowl and cast you all together into the Barathrum.

CARIO. The tongue[463] is given to the herald of these tidings. But on what account, pray, does he purpose to do this to us?

MERCURY. Because you have done the most dreadful of all deeds. For since what time Plutus began to have the use of his eyes as before,[464] no one any longer offers to us gods either[465] frankincense, or laurel, or barley-cake, or victim, or any thing else.

CARIO. No, by Jupiter, nor will he offer them. For you took bad care of us aforetime.[466]

MERCURY. And for the other gods I care less; but I am undone, and am ruined.

CARIO. You’re wise.

MERCURY. For formerly I used to enjoy all good things in the female innkeepers’ shops as soon as it was morning, wine-cake, honey, dried figs, as many as ’tis fitting that Mercury
should eat: but now I go to bed hungry with my legs lying up.[467]

CARIO. Is it not then with justice, who sometimes[468] caused their loss, although you enjoyed such good things.

MERCURY. Ah me, miserable! Ah me, for the cheese-cake[469] that was baked on the fourth day!

CARIO. “You[470] long for the absent, and call in vain.”

MERCURY. Ah me for the ham which I used to devour!

CARIO. Leap upon the bottle[471] there in the open air.[472]

MERCURY. And for the warm entrails which I used to devour!

CARIO. A pain about your entrails seems to torture you.[473]

MERCURY. Ah me, for the cup that was mixed[474] half-and-half!

CARIO. You cannot be too quick in drinking this[475] besides and running away.

MERCURY. Would you assist your own friend in any way?

CARIO. Yes; if you want any of those things in which[476] I am able to assist you.

MERCURY. If you were to procure me a well-baked loaf and give it me to eat, and a huge[477] piece of meat from the sacrifices you are offering within.

CARIO. But there is no carrying out.[478]

MERCURY. And yet whenever you stole[479] any little vessel from your master, I always used[480] to cause you to be undetected.

CARIO. On condition that you also shared yourself, you house-breaker. For a well-baked cake used to come to you.

MERCURY. And then you used to devour this yourself.

CARIO. For you had not an equal share[481] of the blows with me, whenever I was caught in any knavery.

MERCURY. Don't bear malice,[482] if you have got possession of Phyle; but, by the gods, receive me as a fellow-inmate.

CARIO. Then will you abandon the gods and stay here?

MERCURY. Yes; for your condition is much better.

CARIO. How then? do you think desertion a fine thing?

MERCURY. Yes; "for his country is every country, wherever a man is well off."[483]

CARIO. What use then would you be to us, if you were here?

MERCURY. Post me beside the door as turnkey.

CARIO. As turnkey? but we have no need of turns.[484]

MERCURY. As merchant,[485] then.

CARIO. But we are rich: what need then for us to maintain a huckstering Mercury?

MERCURY. Well, as deceiver, then.

CARIO. As deceiver? By no means. For we have no need of deception now, but of simple manners.

MERCURY. As conductor, then.

CARIO. But the god now has the use of his eyes; so we shall no longer want a conductor.

MERCURY. Then I will be president of the games. And what further will you say? For this is most convenient for Plutus, to celebrate musical and gymnastic[486] contests.

CARIO. What a good thing it is to have many[487] surnames! for this fellow has found out a scant living for himself by this means. No wonder all the judges often seek eagerly to be inscribed in many letters.[488]

MERCURY. Then shall I go in upon these terms?

CARIO. Aye, and go yourself to the well and wash[489] the puddings, that you may immediately be thought to be serviceable. [Exeunt Mercury and Cario.]

[entering hastily]
PRIEST OF JUPITER. Who can tell me for certain where Chremylus is? [Enter Chremylus.]

CHREMYLUS. What is the matter, my good sir?

PRIEST. Why, what else but[490] bad? For since what time this Plutus began to have the use of his eyes, I perish with hunger. For I have nothing to eat; and that too, though I am the priest[491] of Jupiter the Preserver.

CHREMYLUS. Oh! by the gods, what is the cause?

PRIEST. No one deigns to sacrifice any longer.

CHREMYLUS. On what account?

PRIEST. Because they are all rich. And yet, at that time, when they had nothing, the one, a merchant, used[492] to come and sacrifice some victim for his safety; and some other one, because he had been acquitted on his trial; and some other one used to sacrifice with favourable omens, and invite me too, the priest. But now not even a single person sacrifices any thing at all, or enters the temple, except it be more than a myriad to ease themselves.
Chr. Do you not then receive your lawful share of these?[493]
Priest. Therefore I also am resolved[494] to bid farewell[495] to Jupiter the Preserver and stay here in this place.[496]
Chr. Be of good courage! for it will be well,[497] if the god please. For[498] Jupiter the Preserver is present here, having come of his own accord.
Priest. Then you tell me all good news.
Chr. We will therefore immediately establish—but[499] stay here—Plutus where he was before established, always guarding the inner cell[500] of the goddess. But let some one give me out here[501] lighted torches, that you may hold them and go before the god.
Priest. Yes, by all means we must do this.
Chr. Call Plutus out, some of you.[502] [Enter Old Woman.]

OLD WOMAN. But what am I to do?
Chr. Take the pots with which we are to establish[503] the god, and carry them on your head in a stately manner, for you came yourself with a party-coloured dress on.

OLD WOMAN. But on what account I came?
Chr. All shall be immediately done for you. For the young man shall come to you in the evening.

OLD WOMAN. Well, by Jove, if[504] indeed you promise me that he shall come to me, I'll carry the pots. [Takes up the pots and puts them on her head.]
Chr. (to the spectators). Well now, these pots act very differently from the other pots. For in the other pots the
scum[505] is on the top; but now the pots are on the top of this old woman.

CHORUS. Therefore 'tis fitting that we delay no longer, but go back to the rear; for we must follow after these, singing.[506] [Exeunt omnes.]
